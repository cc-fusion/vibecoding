Northstar field guide / rev 4.2

Read the chain, not just the gauges.

A simplified pressurized-water reactor model. Every control changes a connected physical process.

1. Neutrons and reactivity

Withdrawing control rods raises neutron flux. Soluble boron and xenon absorb neutrons. As fuel heats, negative temperature feedback suppresses the chain reaction. Flux responds with a delayed, gradual kinetic lag.

2. Primary cooling

Primary pumps move high-pressure water through the core and steam generator. Low flow, coolant loss or low pressure reduces heat removal, raises fuel temperature and can damage cladding. Isolate a leaking loop and use ECCS makeup when inventory falls.

3. Steam and power

Feedwater absorbs heat in the steam generator. Steam pressure and condenser condition limit turbine output. Turbine load sets the requested electrical ceiling; synchronize before exporting to the grid.

4. Trips and recovery

SCRAM rapidly inserts the safety rods. The chain reaction stops, but decay heat remains. A station blackout removes powered pump flow until the emergency diesel starts. Do not confuse a shutdown reactor with a cold reactor.

Faults and maintenance

Use the injector to practice feedwater, pump, rod-drive, condenser, turbine, coolant-leak and grid faults. Dispatching a crew costs reserve funds and takes simulated time. ECCS charges are limited.

Campaign and failure

Complete startup, grid-following and blackout shifts to earn operator XP. Spend XP on actuator, pump and xenon certifications. High fuel damage, extreme temperature, empty coolant inventory or an expired objective ends the operation.
