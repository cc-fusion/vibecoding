Lab manual

How the bench works

Reduced units: σ = ε = m = kB = 1. This is a two-dimensional Lennard-Jones fluid with a shifted cutoff, a choice of thermostat, and periodic boundaries. It is a teaching instrument — consistent, visible, and smaller than a production MD code.

Core loop

  1. Press play (the bench starts running). Watch particles move.
  2. Change one instrument: thermostat, piston, ensemble, or ε.
  3. Wait until the data window fills and the status line says ready.
  4. Record. A miss is logged; the clock is the failure condition, not a single bad sample.

Four couplings you can see

  • Thermostat → kinetic energy → phase. In 2D, ⟨KE⟩ per particle is T. Heat a crystal and ψ6 falls.
  • Piston → density → pressure and structure. Compression spikes the virial; expansion opens vapor.
  • Attraction ε → potential energy, RDF, mixing. The well minimum stays at r = 21/6 ≈ 1.122. ε changes depth, not the location of the minimum. εAB changes whether a mixture demixes.
  • Friction and ensemble → diffusion and fluctuations. Langevin noise lets crystals nucleate. Berendsen damps fluctuations. NVE should hold total energy flat. High γ suppresses D.

2D specifics

The liquid–vapor critical point of 2D LJ sits near T* ≈ 0.45, ρ ≈ 0.37. Interfaces need to be colder than that. Melting at high density can pass through a hexatic regime — the badge will say HEXATIC when local order is high but particles still wander. Long-time diffusion in 2D is subtle; the bench fits an intermediate Einstein slope, D = ΔMSD / (4 Δt).

Grading and failure

Success is a recorded sample inside the tolerance band after the data window fills. Failure is the lab clock, thermal runaway (the integrator loses the sample), or a vitrified capstone. Undergraduate widens bands and lengthens the clock. Principal Investigator tightens both and adds apparatus glitches.

Capstone

Confirm a disordered melt, compress toward ρ ≈ 0.82–0.90, and quench near T* = 0.28–0.40. Too cold and the sample becomes a glass: dense, disordered, immobile. A seed starts heterogeneous nucleation but costs grade. Hold the temperature while the gold order spreads.

Explore the well (diagram only)

The marker sits at the potential minimum. Changing ε deepens the well; it does not move the minimum. That is why the first RDF shell of a cold dense fluid stays near 1.12σ.

Shortcuts to remember

  • Gas law bench: dilute, P ≈ ρT, velocity components Gaussian (kurtosis 3).
  • NVE: equilibrate first, then isolate. Drift is per particle.
  • First shell: melt the stretched crystal so neighbors fall into the well.
  • Demixing: lower εAB below εAA and give the sample time to diffuse.
  • Interface: T* below ~0.42, a condensed ribbon, vapor on both sides.
