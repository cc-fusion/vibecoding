SIGNALLER'S HANDBOOK

Everything you need to run the railway.

Your job

Move timetabled trains to their destinations on time. You don't drive trains — you control signals, junction points and priorities. Trains drive themselves, brake for red signals and stop at booked stations.

Blocks & signals

Each section between stations is a block: only one train may occupy it. A signal shows green when the road ahead is clear, yellow for caution, red for stop. Click any signal to hold it at red and stage trains in your chosen order.

Points (◆)

Mill Junction and Riverdale have hand points. Branch trains to North Mine / Harbour Port need the points swung to BRANCH; main-line trains need MAIN. Click the ◆ on the map or use the Control tab. A train waiting on points shows “Waiting: points”.

Meets on single track

Opposing trains cannot share a block. Hold the lower-priority train at a station loop (2 platforms) and let the other pass. Freight is slow and earns well but blocks the line for a long time — expresses lose you the most satisfaction when delayed.

Train actions

Click a train: Hold/Release it at its next opportunity, Expedite (+speed, costs budget), Cancel the service (penalty), call an Engineer to a breakdown, or — dangerously — Proceed at danger past a red into an occupied block. That can cause a collision and end the shift.

Disruptions

Breakdowns stop trains in-section. Debris and maintenance possessions close blocks — click a closed block to dispatch a clearance crew. Passenger surges lengthen station dwells. Storms cut speeds across the network. Warnings appear in Alerts and the Log.

Money & mood

Every arrival earns revenue (freight pays most). Engineers, crews and expediting cost money; the railway also burns operating budget every minute. Satisfaction drifts toward a level set by current delays — keep it above the target and never let it collapse.

Winning & losing

Hit the scenario targets (services completed, punctuality, satisfaction) by the end of the shift for up to ★★★ and reputation to spend on permanent upgrades. You fail early if satisfaction collapses, the budget is exhausted, or a collision occurs.
