1 · Neutronics — rods, boron, delayed precursors and xenon set reactivity (pcm). Cross prompt-critical (+650 pcm) and physics stops negotiating.

3 · Secondary & Grid — the turbine is your heat sink and your income. Trip it and the primary heats up while revenue stops.
4 · Safety & Power — SCRAM, ECCS, diesels and batteries. In a blackout, every pump is a battery decision.

OPERATOR MANUAL

THE CORE LOOP

Rods & boron set reactivity. Near 0 pcm the reactor is critical and power holds. Positive reactivity → power rises exponentially on the delayed-neutron timescale. Rising fuel/coolant temperature subtracts reactivity (your built-in brake), xenon-135 subtracts much more after power changes, and only decays over (compressed) hours. Generate steam, sell power, keep every needle out of the red.

READING THE PLANT

  • Fuel temp >950°C warning, >1100°C damage, ~1800°C meltdown.
  • Pressure follows coolant temperature; >210 bar strains the vessel, >250 bar sustained ruptures it.
  • Level falling = leak. Uncovered fuel overheats fast.
  • Xenon peaks after a shutdown/trip — restarting into the pit needs boron dilution, rods, or patience.

CONTROLS

  • Rod slider/buttons — fast reactivity.
  • Boron — slow shim, ± buttons nudge 40 ppm.
  • Pumps & speed — coolant flow and heat removal.
  • Turbine valve — heat sink + revenue; trips remove both.
  • ECCS — emergency core refill (needs power).
  • Diesel — your lifeline when the grid dies; batteries bridge the gap.
  • Crew jobs (isolate break, repair pump, restore grid) cost money and time.

WHEN IT GOES WRONG

  • Power excursion: insert rods, borate, SCRAM if reactivity stays positive.
  • LOCA: SCRAM, ECCS on, isolate the break, watch level recover.
  • Blackout: SCRAM, start the diesel before the battery dies, restore flow, cool down gently — decay heat still needs removing.
  • Turbine trip: cut power or the primary will heat and pressurise.
