    How Granular Matter Works

    Your job: complete each experiment's objective before the clock runs out. Pour grains, run equipment, and bend four interacting systems to your will — without collapsing the silo.

    1 · Grain physics. Six materials differ in size, density, friction and bounce. Heavy steel shot sinks and punches through beds; powder clings; big seed nuts ride on top when shaken.
    2 · Moisture & cohesion. The moisture slider (and Wet/Dry tools) adds liquid bridges between grains. Piles stand steeper (higher angle of repose) and beds compact harder — but wet grain forms a self-supporting arch over a hopper outlet and flow stops dead. Dry it out or shake it loose.
    3 · Vibration. The shaking table compacts beds (packing fraction rises), drives Brazil-nut segregation (large grains percolate upward), and breaks jams. Side effect: dynamic wall loading. Vibration + a full silo is how walls die.
    4 · Silo pressure (Janssen). Unlike a liquid, grain pressure saturates with depth because walls carry load by friction. Fill deeper and pressure climbs toward a ceiling; exceed the redline and wall integrity drains. Discharge to relieve it.

    Tools: Pour 1 · Wall 2 · Stir 3 · Wet 4 · Dry 5 · Erase 6 · Probe 7. Actions: Space pause, G gate, V vibration, F feeder, S shake pulse, D dry burst, M mute, +/- speed. Touch works with one finger; brush size is in the panel.

    Progression: finishing an experiment earns Research Points (RP) and unlocks the next one. Spend RP on Steel Shot and Biomass Pellets in the material list. Medals are kept per difficulty. The final experiment — Mega-Silo Throughput — combines discharge targets, humidity fronts, feeder surges and a fragile silo.

    Failure: the timer expires, or wall integrity hits zero and the silo bursts. In Free Sandbox there is no failure — chase the milestone list instead.
