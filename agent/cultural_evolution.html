How to play

The big idea

Communities hold a culture: eight traits (subsistence, shelter, pottery, textiles, ritual, kinship, governance, music) and ten basic words. Culture is copied from neighbours and trade partners, favouring practices that pay off in the local environment and those that are prestigious. Copying is imperfect, people invent, borders get fought over, plagues strike, and communities split to found new settlements. Over centuries this produces a branching family tree of cultures.

Your goal

  • Run history until the scenario's final year. Keep the missions (communities alive, word diversity, writing) in view on the Inspect tab.
  • Capstone — reconstruction: at the end, rebuild the true family tree from observed evidence. Join the two most similar communities, repeat, and submit. Score = share of the true clades you recover.
  • Verdict: success needs a strong reconstruction plus at least two missions. Failure happens if civilisation collapses (population falls to a fifth of its peak for 8 years), if culture stagnates (trait diversity below 8% for 40 years), or if the reconstruction recovers less than 35%.

Controls

  • Mouse/touch: click a community to inspect it; click the map with an active tool to use it. Hover for quick info.
  • Keyboard: Space play/pause · 1–4 speed (0.5×, 1×, 2×, 4×) · Q inspect · T trade route · S sponsor · R relief · A academy · C colony · V oral survey · O cycle overlay · M mute · H help · Esc cancel tool / menu.
  • The game pauses automatically when you switch browser tabs.

Systems and how they connect

  • Contact: neighbouring territories share borders; mountains weaken contact and rivers strengthen it. Trade routes add long-distance contact. Contact drives trait and word copying.
  • Environment selection: grassland, forest, desert and mountain reward different subsistence and shelter practices. Droughts shift the payoffs (irrigation pays in dry years) and shrink carrying capacity.
  • Prestige: prosperous, innovative, trading and large communities become models others copy and are more likely to win wars.
  • Small populations forget: below about 12k people, writing fades and complex practices can be lost.
  • Language: words drift through sound changes; writing slows drift; borrowing across borders confuses family relations; conquest erases the conquered tongue.
  • Growth and fission: communities grow toward carrying capacity, then send settlers to found new communities, creating a new branch of the tree.
  • Economy: Cultural Capital comes from trade and prestige; Research Points come from invention and scholarship and unlock tools.

Tools

  • Trade Route (12 CC): link two communities. Boosts contact, prestige and income; can carry plague.
  • Sponsor Practice (10 CC): make a chosen trait variant prestigious in one community for 40 years.
  • Relief Aid (14 CC): send grain to recover population after famine or plague.
  • Scribal Academy (20 CC, 45 RP): boosts writing for 30 years.
  • Found Colony (25 CC, 20 RP): send settlers to an empty plot next to a large community. Creates a new lineage.
  • Oral Survey (12 CC, 80 RP): collect an oral history revealing two sister communities.

Policies

  • Open Exchange: contact ×1.3. Tolerance Edict: conquest chance ×0.35. Military Levy: conflict ×2.2 and more prestige for victors. Cultural Standards: majority practices spread faster, but innovation slows and diversity can collapse.

Tips

  • Trait variants that match the land spread quickly; watch the Phylogeny tab to see which communities are converging.
  • Colonies create new branches. Good for diversity; they are small and vulnerable at first.
  • Use the Language overlay to see cognates: communities sharing a word colour share ancestry or borrowing.
